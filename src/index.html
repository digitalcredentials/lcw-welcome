Your credentials, in a space you own

Built so your credentials stay yours, even if an app shuts down.

Learner Credential Wallet keeps your digital diplomas, certificates, and badges in a personal Portable Web Space: storage that belongs to you, not to the app.

How it works

Most wallet apps keep your credentials in an account the app controls, so if the app shuts down, you can lose access to your credentials. Learner Credential Wallet keeps your credentials in a Portable Web Space instead: storage that belongs to you, not to the app. Your wallet opens that space. Think of email: a provider stores your mail, but the mail belongs to you. You can read your mail in any mail app, or move your mailbox to another provider. For the alpha, we keep your space on our servers, and you can also open it with another wallet built for Portable Web Spaces. Choosing where your space is kept comes next.

Frequently asked questions

Quick answers about the alpha, the wallet, and your Portable Web Space.

What is the alpha, and how do I get access?

The alpha is an early test version of Learner Credential Wallet. Use it with the test badge we send you, to see how the wallet and your Portable Web Space work together. Don't use it for real records: it may stop working, and when the alpha ends, your account and everything in it are deleted. We'll email you the date well in advance, so you can download a copy first. A version you can use for your real credentials is planned for early 2027.

Request early access, and we'll email you a code that's just for you. If lots of people sign up at once, you may wait until we let the next group in.

I already use the Learner Credential Wallet phone app. What changes for me?

Nothing changes for now. The alpha is a separate test, so keep using the phone app for your real credentials. Schools that send credentials to the phone app keep sending them there.

What is a Portable Web Space?

It's where your credentials are kept: storage that belongs to you, not to the wallet app. The wallet is how you open it, the way any mail app can open your mailbox.

For the alpha, we store your space on our servers. You can open it with Learner Credential Wallet or, in Settings, connect another wallet built for Portable Web Spaces. Choosing where your space is kept comes next. See how it works.

Which devices and browsers can I use?

The wallet runs in your web browser, so there's nothing to install. It works best on a computer for now.

Should I sign in with a password or a passkey?

Either works. A passkey lets you sign in with your fingerprint, face, or device PIN instead of typing a password. Either way, closing the tab signs you out. On a browser where you've added a passkey, your fingerprint, face, or PIN opens the wallet again.

After you sign in with a password, the wallet offers to add a passkey. Some browsers and password managers can't make the kind of passkey the wallet needs. If yours can't, the wallet tells you, and you can keep using your password.

How do I add a credential?

In the wallet, choose Add Credential. You can paste the credential, choose or drag in its file, or scan its QR code with your camera or from a picture.

You can also get a credential straight from an issuer's website. When the site asks which wallet to use, choose Learner Credential Wallet. That's how your test badge arrives.

The wallet is built for credentials that follow open standards: W3C Verifiable Credentials, including Open Badges 3.0.

Why am I asked to allow the wallet, and then to choose a wallet when I claim my badge?

They're two different steps. Allowing happens once: it adds Learner Credential Wallet to your browser's list of wallets. Choosing happens each time a website offers you a credential, so you decide which wallet takes it. When you claim your test badge, choose Learner Credential Wallet.

Why does the wallet ask me to sign in again when I save a credential?

When a website sends you a credential, the wallet asks you to confirm it's you before saving it, even if you're already signed in. That way, no one else can add credentials to a wallet you've left open. The box says "Sign in to continue," but you haven't been signed out.

Why doesn't the claim page take me to my wallet afterward?

The claim page doesn't know which wallet you chose, so it can't send you there. Once it says your badge is claimed, open your wallet to see it.

What's the "Credential JSON" box in Add Credential for?

A credential is a block of text in a format called JSON. When you choose a file, drag one in or scan a QR code, the wallet shows that text in the box, so you can check it before adding it. If someone gave you the text instead of a file, paste it there. Don't edit it: a credential is signed, so any change means it won't show as verified.

Can I keep a copy of my credentials?

Yes. In the wallet, open Settings and choose Download my data. It saves your credentials in one file, with their tags. To bring them back, choose Import my data and pick that file.

The file isn't encrypted, so keep it somewhere safe.

What does "verified" mean?

When you open a credential, the wallet checks that the issuer signed it and it hasn't been changed since, that it hasn't expired, and that the issuer hasn't revoked it. It also looks the issuer up in lists of known issuers.

If you see "We couldn't confirm who issued this," the credential itself may be fine. Some of those lists can't be checked from a browser yet.

How do I share a credential, and can I stop sharing it?

Open the credential, choose Share, then Anyone with the link. You get a link to send, a QR code, and an option to add it to LinkedIn. People who open the link see that credential and whether it checks out, and nothing else in your space.

To stop sharing, choose Only you, then Take it down. The link and QR code stop working, including for anyone you've already sent them to.

Is my data private?

The wallet encrypts what you add in your browser, before it's stored in your space, so our storage servers hold only an encrypted copy. A credential you share is the exception: anyone with the link can see it until you take it down. We're still improving how this works. See the privacy notice.

The architecture behind the alpha

The three open specifications the wallet and your Portable Web Space are built on.

  • Portable Web Spaces

    The storage your credentials live in, which belongs to you. Formerly Wallet Attached Storage.

    SpecificationRepository

  • Authorization Capabilities (ZCAP)

    The permissions the wallet uses to open your space: signed requests that show it's allowed to read or write what's in it.

    SpecificationRepository

  • Encrypted Data Vaults

    Storage that holds only encrypted data. The wallet uses it to encrypt what you add in your browser, before it's stored in your space.

    SpecificationRepository
